# Fact-checking

A reader trusts a post as much as its least-checked claim. Check each one, and report each that does not hold up, with where it is.

## Find the claims

Read the change and list every claim a reader could check:

- names of people, places, products and organisations, and their titles;
- dates, numbers, prices and percentages;
- quotations, and who said them;
- statements of cause and effect ("X led to Y") and of firsts and bests ("the first", "the largest").

Opinions the post presents as the writer's own need no source.

## Check each one

For each claim, find its source: a link beside it, the list of sources at the end of the post, the issue, or a file in the repository. Then:

1. **There is a source.** A claim with none fails.
2. **The source says it.** Read the part of the source the claim rests on. A figure must match, with its unit and its date; a quotation must match word for word, with any cut shown by an ellipsis.
3. **It is still true.** A figure "as of" a date says so; a post that says "now" or "currently" names when.
4. **It is said fairly.** The claim does not go further than the source, such as "most" for "some".

When a source is a web page you cannot open from the sandbox, say so for that claim in your report, rather than passing or failing it.

## Report

For each claim that fails, give its file and line, what it says, what the source says (or that it has none), and what would fix it. Never change the post's words yourself: the writer fixes them.
